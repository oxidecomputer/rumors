# Holistic review of the before and suanpan crates

This note is the entry document of a multi-agent review of two crates in the `rumors` workspace at commit `9e5784fb4dce977cfbdfd1619886d1482b5ce764`. Reviewed: `before` (`crates/before`: Interval Tree Clocks over a packed skyline coding, with `src/`, `tests/`, `benches/`, `examples/`, `build.rs`, `Cargo.toml`, the `docs/` widget assets, the `reference/` transcription of the ITC paper, and the four detached workspaces `fuzz/`, `fuzzfit/`, `wasm32-pins/`, and `surfacecheck/`), `crates/before-fuelscape` (the detached fuelscape pipeline), `crates/suanpan` (cliff-free signed accumulators, `before`'s arithmetic substrate), `crates/surface-scan`, and the recipes that verify them (the root `justfile`, `tools/`, `.cargo/mutants.toml`, `.github/workflows`). Not reviewed: the `rumors` crate itself, except as `before`'s consumer (the dependence document carries that ledger; findings about `rumors` belong to the separate rumors review), `crates/before-viz`, and `crates/rumors-tracing`. No benchmark and no amplification-board run was made for any finding. The one full test-suite timing (the suite-economics sweep) ran while another review workflow shared the machine, at load averages between 3.8 and 19.9, so its per-test times are indicative rather than quiet-machine numbers. At assembly time the working tree's HEAD was `7440d1a3`, two commits above the briefed commit; I confirmed with read-only git that `9e5784fb` is an ancestor, that both later commits touch only `.agent-notes/`, that the in-scope diffstat is empty, and that the tree is clean, so every line number in the note holds at both commits.

The review produced 1,204 findings in eight class documents beside this file (16 high, 211 medium, 574 low, 403 nit), from 34 partition reports and 13 sweeps. The witness pass constructed and ran tests for 117 of them in isolated worktrees: 89 constructions demonstrated the finding as stated, 26 were inconclusive for platform or permission reasons, and 2 refuted a clause of the finding (both kept, with the refutation recorded beside the entry). A later 32-bit pass re-ran the four constructions that had needed a 32-bit target in the tree's own `wasm32-pins` executor and demonstrated all four, so the final tally is 93 demonstrated, 22 inconclusive, 2 refuted. The counts in this note are recomputed from the review's `evidence/index/counts.tsv` and from `evidence/witness.md`; the eight documents report the same totals.

## Verdict

Both crates are built the way their owner's doctrine asks, and the review found that the discipline holds where it matters most. No production kernel in `before` returns a wrong answer on a 64-bit target: the party, version, rank, skyline, comparison, watermark, fill-and-grow, and codec partitions each traced their kernels arm by arm, every `expect`, `unreachable!`, and `panic!` message in shipped code reads as a one-line proof (the inventory sweep counts 143 `expect` sites, 34 `unreachable!`, one `panic!`, and zero `unwrap()` outside doctests), with two exceptions the correctness document names: `expect("freeze count fits u32")` in `EpochLedger::epoch` states its conclusion rather than its premise (skyline-query-24), and `expect("site count fits u32")` in the fill walk's frame ledger is a production panic reachable by input scale (inventory-1, skyline-fill-grow-23); the recursion sweep's call-graph scan finds no library function recursing on input depth, though the depth-100k proof drives a listed subset of operations over one left-only spine and the public shape iterators appear in no deep test (clock-22, recursion-6, meter-adequacy-1); and canonical form as byte equality is argued at `skyline.rs:65-90` and pinned from every side (strict reject corpus, mutant re-derivation through the oracle bridge, `fuzz_decode`'s byte-identity assertion, the fuelscape grammar-versus-decoder census), with two qualifications: the admission walk's mid-stream collapsible-pair rejection has no committed witness through `Span::decode` (skyline-coding-6, demonstrated: with the arm's `zero_delta` forced false, a non-canonical `hi` is accepted while 227 tests stay green), and `fuzz_decode` runs only at `just all` cadence, outside the gate (fuzz-guests-pins-38). `suanpan`'s kernel was traced by hand at every arm the review touched and is reviewably correct on 64-bit targets; its crate page is the strongest public prose in the two crates. The instruments' cores are sound and in places exemplary: the board pairs every judgment leg with a committed known-bad artifact convicted through `evaluate` itself, floors are derived from operands rather than readings, `ByCurrency` makes a new currency a compile error at every site, the envelope suite states fused-walk costs as relational identities that cannot rot, `laws!` and `diff_ops!` make registration structural, and the fuzz seed corpus has one derivation shared by `#[path]` between writer and checker.

The kernel's verdict is two-sided. Its values are right; its cost contracts are not all honored, and its width boundaries are unguarded on 32-bit targets. Five constructed tests demonstrate work over the published contract on production paths: `Version::join` on a flat wide leaf against a spine of pairs is Θ(depth × width) because the builder's amortization never prices the re-flush of a re-anchored wide code (skyline-coding-9); `Ranked::cmp` publishes `O(|self| + |other|)` while running the multiplication-bound settle (rank-33; the construction demonstrated that the co-sweep pays the settle, at 0.77× `rank`'s limb operations and about 1.08× its wall time at every scale in the same run, while the superlinear order itself stands by reading: the limb meter prices a multiplication by its operand limbs and read 0.989, and the 1.14 to 1.28 wall exponent the construction also printed was taken under undisclosed load and is not a measurement); the masked comparison behind `OwnVersion` re-peeks a stationary cursor's trailing run every round (skyline-sweep-place-masked-5); `Query::coverage` sweeps the clamped endpoint once per surviving hole (span-causally-36); and the tuple-literal constructors re-copy and re-validate every subtree per nesting level (party-11, version-core-16, clock-14). The distinct-minima memo families hold 50 to 105 heap bytes per input byte on `tick` against the crate's small-constant promise (skyline-fill-grow-2). Under `lib.rs:350-353`, which makes every asymptotic claim a hard guarantee for all input sizes, each of these is a contract breach, and `tests/meter.rs:7-9` says in the same present tense that the implementation is "far from" linear; the five constructed rows say which side is stale for those operations: the header's sentence is true of them today, and the hard-guarantee sentence stays false until each is fixed. The owner has ruled that the guarantee is the contract as intended and holds absolutely (`triage/rulings.md`, ruling 1): each of the five is a defect to fix, never an exception to declare, and the instruments come first, so that every breach reads as a failure before its cure lands. The correctness defects proper are all width boundaries: `suanpan`'s wide-operand digit positions wrap on a 32-bit release build with no trap (suanpan-24, the one high-severity production defect), the fork iterators' `len()` panics past `usize` on wasm32 (clock-17, party-13), the fill walk's `u32` link ledger panics on a canonical multi-GiB input (inventory-1), and the wide-gamma width guard admits 32 values past dashu's capacity on 32-bit targets (codec-bits-23). The review's 32-bit pass ran the four 32-bit constructions in the tree's own `wasm32-pins` executor and observed each: the unchecked guest returns the wrong values 5 and 1 for suanpan-24 where the `# Panics` contract promises a panic, `forks(1u64 << 32).len()` traps on both `Clock` and `Party` while `forks(2^32 - 1).len()` returns `usize::MAX`, and the borsh decode of a `2^32 - 32`-zero gamma prefix traps where `2^32 - 33` returns a typed error. The tree's committed guest names none of them, so none has a first-party pin, and inventory-1 remains settled by reading, since its trigger is a multi-GiB input.

The owner asked whether the verification apparatus is justified by what it catches. For most of it the answer is yes, with the catch nameable: the worst-cases pin recorded a heap regression on two families that the envelope suite's limb-only bounds would not have caught (f34f4b34), the committed known-bad oracles and kernels are convicted through the same checkers that judge production and so demonstrate that each criterion can fail, and the witness pass, which set out to refute, refuted clauses of only two entries out of 117 while confirming 93 (89 in the main pass, four more in the 32-bit pass). Three groups of exceptions carry the apparatus's weight of findings. First, instruments that cannot fail: the stack-segments currency is judged on every board cell and pinned at zero on all 84 envelope rows, but its only writer is `#[cfg(test)] fn grow`, unreachable from every binary that reads it (six partitions and sweeps filed it independently); the scan column has no liveness floor in any envelope table; two flatness suites return growth 1.0 on a zero counter; three rosters attest that a kernel is named rather than run; and four empty acceptance buffers exist for failures that have never arrived. Second, instruments that drive the benign case: the join envelopes exercise only the absorb face, the `rank_sum` pins fix the cheap order while naming it the adversary, every committed query has at most one hole, the board's membership row measures its early exit on 28 of 29 families and its covers row on all 14, and the fuelscape overlay feeds byte-identical self-pairs to eight rows whose kernel opens with `canonical_eq`. This is why the five cost breaches above were invisible; the meters that should have caught them were pointed at shapes on which the claims are true, and two denomination blind spots (the limb meter charges a multiplication by its operand limbs, so an `M(n)` term reads linear; no currency counts a stack-word read) mean the bench judge's wall exponent was the only committed instrument that could have seen two of them. Third, machinery that outlived the constraint that justified it: the line-scan extractor (surfacecheck catches strictly more), four envelope harnesses for one measurement, the oracle's two verbatim copies of production's balanced fold, the presize and `display_growth` A/B arms with no recorded verdict, `emit_probe.rs` with its `bitvec` dev-dependency, `spanbands`, the cliff-fan family, the `decided` dates, and `EXEMPTIONS`/`ITEM_EXCEPTIONS`. The simplification document's dissolvable-scaffolding section lists thirty-three such entries, each with the failure class it catches and the instrument that replaces it. The recommendation is to keep the architecture, dissolve the listed scaffolding, and add the floors and the adversarial shapes that the constructions name.

Four themes recur across every document. Prose from before the 2026-07-25 flag day (faf3cd0a, which made the skyline coding the stored form) still describes the previous world as the present in the envelope header, `meter/tier2.rs`, `testing/compactness.rs`, and the `Packed` docs; the removal of `Batch`, the landing of surfacecheck, and the `cfg(test)` gating of `grow` left similar residue. Contracts that `rumors` relies on are stated in tests rather than at the method: tick region-locality, join/meet subadditivity, and the coincident span's one-comparison cost live in `laws.rs`, a test-module constant, and code comments, while the public rustdoc stops short, so the consumer cites test names. And a small vocabulary (door at 279 lines, seam 276, honest 234, mint 82, em-dashes in 374 `//` lines) is used crate-wide without a defining site; every partition that met it asked for one owner ruling rather than local edits. Ghost references to deleted code, which the crate's own `AGENTS.md` forbids, account for most of the documentation highs: the retired `before::implementation` essay is cited from eight places, and `party.rs` names an `EvNode`, an `IdLit`, and a removed `compare`.

## How this review was done

The review ran in stages, each leaving its artifacts in the review scratchpad. An orientation agent wrote the map (`evidence/map.md`): the layering of the shipped code, the data flow of the four core operations, the invariants and quantitative promises with their anchors, the instrument table, a script-generated module dependency graph (`map/depgraph.py`), a glossary with defining files, and the partition list with each partition's neighbors. The map's dependency graph and its grep-verified claims (no production code names an instrument module; `descend!` is used only by `testing/bridge.rs` and test files) were executed; the rest is reading.

The code was divided into 34 partitions, listed in the quality map above, and every partition file was read in full with line numbers: 137,630 lines in the partition files themselves, plus the out-of-partition anchors each finding rests on (the partition summaries in `evidence/partitions/<key>.md` name them). Two small in-scope files sit outside the partition rosters by name and are accounted for here: `crates/before/src/codec.rs`, the codec module root (80 lines), is inside the codec-bits partition's fourteen-file full read (its stated 2,995 lines reproduce only with `codec.rs` counted); `.github/workflows/pages.yml` (89 lines) was read in full by no partition (the gate-legs sweep read `ci.yml` in full, and the prose-hygiene sweep cites `pages.yml` once in an excerpt), so the reconciliation pass read it whole: it deploys the out-of-scope `before-viz` visualizer to GitHub Pages on pushes touching `crates/before/**`, runs no verification recipe, and is outside the gate's path; `tools/workflowlint` holds its `uses:` pins as it does `ci.yml`'s. Each partition was read by three or four independent lens agents. Kernel partitions (crate-root, clock, party, version-core, rank, span-causally, the five skyline partitions, the two codec partitions, suanpan) had four lenses: structure, prose, correctness, and claims. Instrument partitions (oracle-laws, meter-core, meter-registry-tier2, the three board partitions, surface-roster, testing-oracles, testing-diff-gen, the two envelope halves, tests-other, benches-examples, fuzz-guests-pins, the two fuzzfit partitions, the two fuelscape partitions, and tools) had scaffolding, adequacy, structure-prose, and instrument-correctness. The one pure test partition (suanpan-tests) had structure-prose, blind-spots, and api-economics. Lens agents read; a few ran a Python transcription of an arithmetic claim (the board's `judge::trend` fit, `mechanism()`'s substring tests) and none ran cargo.

The `tools/` directory was at first covered only by the gate-legs sweep, which read it in ranges. After the class documents were assembled, the completeness critic found that no partition had read it in full, so it was reviewed as a thirty-fourth partition (4,845 lines in fifteen files, plus roughly 700 lines of the sites its findings rest on), with the same four instrument lenses, the same refutation and history passes, and the same finalization as the other instrument partitions. The partition postdates the witness pass, so none of its 33 entries carries a Witness line. They were merged into the class documents afterwards, each under a `Workspace tools (tools/)` heading or into the existing gate-recipes section, with the Counts tables recomputed; at merge time every line-numbered excerpt was checked mechanically against `9e5784fb` (207 excerpts across the 33 entries), and three entries whose excerpt numbers were one line off (tools-5, tools-13, tools-14; the quoted text verbatim, the numbers corrected and the correction stated in a synthesis note) were the only defects found.

The note is a directory. `README.md` is this file. The eight class documents each carry every finding of their classes (in the finalizers' template, except that `documentation.md`, `simplification.md`, and `verification.md` carry their nits as one-line table rows), a highest-value list, crate-wide patterns, per-module sections in the crates' order, positives, open questions with recommendations, and counts: `correctness.md` (51 entries), `claims.md` (75; asymptotics, space, canonicality, derivations, paper fidelity), `verification.md` (254; verification gaps and test quality), `simplification.md` (376; simplification, modularity, vestigial code, idiom, and the dissolvable-scaffolding section), `documentation.md` (399), `performance.md` (24), `api.md` (21; API suggestions and feature gaps, each with an adopt/consider/decline disposition), and `dependence.md` (4, plus the 47-row ledger of what `rumors` relies on and the *Costs rumors relies on* table beside it). The evidence behind them: `evidence/partitions/<key>.md` (the 34 finalized partition reports, with their positives, open questions, and dropped lists), `evidence/sweeps/<key>.md` (the 13 finalized sweep reports), `evidence/map.md` (the orientation map), and `evidence/witness.md` when present (the constructed tests, commands, and outputs verbatim). `triage/rulings.md` records the owner's rulings on the review's questions, one numbered entry per decision; an entry a ruling disposes carries a Disposition line citing it.

Ids are `<partition or sweep key>-<n>`, the finalizers' own numbering, stable across every document; the same id names the same finding everywhere, and a finding appears in full only in the document of its primary class, with cross-references by id elsewhere. Severity is a four-step scale whose reading the class document states at its head: high (a stated contract broken on a reachable input, a gate of record failing, a hard rule of the tree breached, or an enforced instrument passing an artifact of the class it exists to catch), medium (a contract clause breached on a constructible input, an instrument weaker than its documentation says at a place a regression would land, or prose a maintainer would act on and be wrong), low (a narrower breach, a corner, or a bounded legibility or maintenance cost with a fixed-sign fix), nit (a one-line edit). Provenance uses four words: demonstrated (a constructed test ran and showed the behavior), executed (a run settled a number without a constructed mutant), verified (mechanically checked or re-derived by grep, git, arithmetic, or a hand trace against the cited lines), assessed (read). Where the witness pass touched an entry, a Witness line adds its outcome: demonstrated, inconclusive (the construction needed a command the agent could not run, so the entry stands on its reading), or refuted (the construction contradicted a clause; the entry is kept and the note says which clause fell). Owner-gated marks every entry that touches the stable public API or reopens a recorded ruling; such entries are suggestions.

Start from a class document's highest-value list, which is ordered by severity and confidence and names the ids; the per-module sections carry the full records, and the Owner decisions section above is the consolidated list of what needs a ruling. A reader checking one finding should open its entry, confirm the quoted evidence at the cited lines (every line number holds at `9e5784fb`), and then, if a Witness line is present, read the construction in `evidence/witness.md`.

## Limits

The review did not run the amplification board, any benchmark, the bench judge, the coverage recipes, the mutation campaign, the fuzz targets, the fuzz-fit harness, or the wasm32-pins leg; every finding about those instruments rests on reading their code and their committed data, on the sweeps' scripts, and on the witness constructions that a filtered `cargo nextest` could reach. Consequently the review cannot say what allocates under coverage instrumentation in `masked_cmp_hole_envelope` (gate-legs-1), whether the fuzzfit gate stream has been running (fuzzfit-strategies-13), or whether the bench judge's current SKIP set and the board's `version_eq` readings are what the code implies. Which of `lib.rs:350-353` and `tests/meter.rs:7-9` is the stale present-tense statement is not among these open facts: the five constructed rows settle it for five operations (the header's sentence is true of them today), and the board could not have settled it either way, because its committed families are the shapes on which the claims hold, so a green board is consistent with the breaches.

A 32-bit run was not permitted under the main run's caps: the tree's 32-bit executor is the detached `wasm32-pins` workspace (a wasm32 guest under wasmtime, resolved in its own `Cargo.lock`), and the witness agents could not build detached workspaces. It was run afterwards, in that executor, by the 32-bit pass: the four 32-bit correctness findings (suanpan-24, clock-17 and party-13, codec-bits-23) were each demonstrated as their entries predict (suanpan-24 as a wrong value with no trap in the unchecked guest and as a trap in the checked one; `forks(1u64 << 32).len()` trapping at exactly `k = 2^32`; the gamma guard trapping at `2^32 - 32` zeros and returning a typed error at `2^32 - 33`). The guest has no stderr, so each trap's panic message and site remain attributed by reading, as the entries say. The `u32` link cap (inventory-1) is still settled by reading, with the mechanism confirmed consistent and the runtime effect unobserved, because its trigger is a multi-GiB input.

The one full suite timing ran on a loaded machine (load average never below 3.8) with another workflow holding the build-directory lock part of the time, so no wall-time number in the note is a quiet-machine number; the deterministic counter readings the witness pass quotes are immune to load.

The witness pass's ad hoc mutation campaign was small (a handful of mutants per agent, batched); its two survivors are confirmed, but it says nothing about the other 54,918 listed mutants. Two witness outcomes refuted a clause (surface-roster-6's exposure clause; board-ops-render-26's numbers, which were the earlier commit's), and four narrowed an entry (skyline-sweep-place-masked-20, codec-bits-15, meter-registry-tier2-7, and rank-4, whose byte-level clause the construction struck while confirming the bit-level one); the entries are kept with the qualification beside them.

Whether off-tree measurements exist behind the front page's "100×" and "100 parties and 1,000,000 events" figures, behind the `MinWeb::compacting` ratios, or behind the probe-build adequacy claims is a question only the owner can answer; the review records what the tree does and does not hold.

The dependence ledger records value guarantees; the high-severity findings that touch `rumors` are all cost claims (join, masked comparison, coverage, the rank log factor). The dependence document's table *Costs rumors relies on* carries one row per such operation with `before`'s cost contract, the demonstrated exception, and `rumors`' pricing site as far as the sweep recorded it; where the sweep recorded no `rumors` budget for an operation the table says so, and filling those cells is work for the rumors review. `rumors`' own code was read only at its use sites, and the three observations carried to the rumors review were not verified here.

Duplicates across partitions and sweeps were kept as separate entries with cross-references rather than merged, because each carries its own anchors and acceptance clause; the counts by document therefore overstate distinct defects (the correctness document, for example, counts 51 entries and 45 distinct defects). The `evidence/` layout named above is assembled from the review's finalized reports; anchors in the class documents were spot-checked against the tree by each synthesis agent (between sixteen and all of them per document) and none needed correction, but no synthesis agent re-verified every anchor of every entry. The 33 `tools/` entries merged after assembly are the exception: every one of their 207 line-numbered excerpts was compared mechanically against `9e5784fb`, and the three off-by-one runs found (tools-5, tools-13, tools-14) were corrected in the merged copies with the correction stated beside each entry.

Paths written as `<session scratchpad>/...` in the evidence files name run logs and scratch tests kept in the review session's scratch directory, which is not committed; the witness file quotes the decisive output of each run, and the worktree paths it names were removed after the runs.
