# Triage plan for the before and suanpan review

This addendum proposes how to work through the review's 1,204 entries with
Finch's judgment applied where it changes the outcome and nowhere else. It
is the sibling of the rumors review's plan
(`../2026-09-01-holistic-review-rumors/TRIAGE.md`): the same record, the
same dispositions, the same working protocol, adapted to what is different
here. Three things are different. Two rulings already exist and fix the
order of the most important work: instrument first, then cure, for every
demonstrated cost and space breach. The public API is declared stable, so
every API entry is a suggestion, adopted only on explicit direction. And the
review's third theme, verification machinery that outlived its constraint,
is large enough (thirty-three dissolvable-scaffolding entries) to be its own
phase with its own discipline.

## The shape in one paragraph

The unit of triage is the ruling and the lane, not the finding. The
README's 92 owner decisions already group 338 entries, and the class
documents' crate-wide patterns group 499 more into about fifty named
classes, each disposed by one ruling or one sweep. What remains are 544
entries only a per-module lane reaches, most low or nit, each carrying its
own stated resolution and acceptance. Ordering follows the rulings and the
dependencies: the instruments first (the breaching-shape rows rulings 1 and
2 call for, the floors, the early-exit rows, the gate's own provenance
holes, and the meter-surface stability question that decides how a dozen
entries are classified); then the correctness and cost-contract cures, each
landing after its instrument reads red; then the vocabulary rulings and
their sweeps; then the pattern sweeps (ghost references and flag-day prose
first, since the former breach a hard rule); then the scaffolding
dissolution, each retirement preceded by the replacement that demonstrably
catches what the old instrument caught; then the module lanes; then the API
suggestions as one block; then the measured performance work; and last the
items carried to the rumors review. Triage closes when `ledger.py check`
reports zero pending and zero defects.

## Dispositions

Every row ends in exactly one of these. The vocabulary is the rumors plan's,
with one addition to the never-defer set: entries of class `claim` (a cost
or space contract the code does not honor) are correctness by ruling 1 and
cannot be deferred either.

| Disposition | Meaning | Terminal when |
|---|---|---|
| `fix` | Land the entry's stated Resolution, meeting its Acceptance. | `sha` names the landed commit; pending until then. |
| `fix-amended` | Land a resolution Finch amended. | `sha` and a ruling carrying the amendment. |
| `model` | The behavior is intended. The ruling names where the intent is stated so the finding cannot recur. | A ruling with a named home. |
| `defer` | A design proposal or a resource trade that waits on a measurement or a later phase. The ruling names the home. | A ruling with a named home. Never for high severity, `correctness`, or `claim`. |
| `dispute` | Finch refutes the finding. The ruling records why. | A ruling. |
| `dup` | Fully carried by another entry. | A ruling or the `note` column naming the entry of record. |

`ledger.py check` distinguishes pending (open, or `fix` without a sha) from
defects (a forbidden disposition, a `model` with no ruling, an id missing
from the ledger). Both must be zero to close.

## The record

`triage/ledger.tsv` has a row per id. Seed columns, regenerated by
`ledger.py seed` from the documents: document, severity, class, provenance,
witness outcome, owner-gating, module heading, the crate-wide patterns the
entry belongs to, the README owner decisions and highest-value items that
cite it, a proposed phase, and a proposed lane for module-lane entries.
Triage columns, filled by hand: `disposition`, `ruling`, `sha`, `note`.
Reseeding preserves the triage columns; a `note` beginning `phase!` pins a
hand-set phase and lane.

`triage/rulings.md` already exists with rulings 1 and 2 in its own format
(`## Ruling <n> (<date>): <title>`, then Disposes, Finch's words where
quoted, Decision, What follows). New rulings append in that format; the
ledger's `ruling` column cites the number. The README says an entry a
ruling disposes carries a Disposition line; the ledger row is that line's
mechanical form, and whether the class documents also get the line is
process question 1 below.

The three checks that make the record trustworthy are the rumors plan's:
coverage (every id has a row and `check` fails otherwise), provenance (a
`fix` needs a sha the coordinator has verified against the entry's
Acceptance), and remainder (every crate-wide sweep lands with its
regenerating grep or a `tools/` check run to zero in the same series).
