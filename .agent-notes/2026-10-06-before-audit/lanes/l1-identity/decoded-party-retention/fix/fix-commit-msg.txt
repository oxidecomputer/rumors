before: Decode each part into its own exactly sized buffer

Previously a decoded clock, span, or ranked key adopted its input as one
`Bytes` and sliced it: the party and version (or the two endpoints, or
the version behind a rank prefix) each pinned the whole input buffer plus
a shared header. A part handed on alone, after `into_parts`, `tick`,
`forks(0)`, a failed `join`, or `Party::without`, kept its siblings'
bytes alive: up to the whole input, or twice it after a stream reader's
doubling. Standalone decodes adopted the read vector with its spare
capacity, so a 3-byte party read from a slice occupied 32 bytes.

Now `Bits` has no constructor that takes a `Bytes`: it adopts an owned
vector, sealed to its length, or static bytes. Every decode entry point
takes an owned `Vec<u8>`; a composite validates its head and splits the
vector with `split_off`, so each part owns storage sized to its own
encoding and no decode path can produce a slice of a shared buffer. A
decoded point span still stores its endpoint once, as `Span::at` does.
Hexadecimal text decodes into a vector allocated once at its exact
length, so sealing it never reallocates.
